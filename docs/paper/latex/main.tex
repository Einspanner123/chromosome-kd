\begin{abstract}
Automated karyotyping requires reliable detection of approximately $46$
chromosomes from $24$ morphologically similar classes in each metaphase image.
Small objects, touching or overlapping instances, weak boundaries, and
near-saturated class confidence make precise localization and reliable ranking
particularly difficult. We present
\emph{KaryoFlow}, a detector designed for this regime through
low-dimensional rectified box transport, identity-consistent refinement of
adjacent proposals, and final localization-quality calibrated ranking. On two
public chromosome datasets with markedly different object-scale distributions,
KaryoFlow attains the highest observed mAP: it improves on the strongest
evaluated detector by $2.2\%$ on the small-object-heavy cohort and reaches a
comparable point estimate (within $0.0001$) on the scale-shifted cohort. Combining few-step inference with cascade-
head distillation provides up to $2.73\times$ faster inference than the
RF+Heun reference, while distillation reduces mAP by only $0.0003$ relative to
its six-head teacher. These results establish a localization-aware generative
approach and provide a new direction for high-precision chromosome detection.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Rectified Flow, object detection, localization quality, diffusion models, medical image analysis, chromosome karyotyping

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{C}{hromosome} karyotyping remains a labor-intensive step in
cytogenetic analysis.
Its detection stage is structurally unusual: a metaphase spread contains a
nearly fixed set of about $46$ chromosomes assigned to $24$ classes, many
instances occupy less than one percent of the image, and neighboring
chromosomes frequently overlap in their bounding-box support. In our two
held-out cohorts, the median instance count is $46$; $99.1\%$ and $100\%$ of
images contain at least one pair of ground-truth boxes with IoU above $0.1$.
The median relative box area also shifts from $0.4\%$ to $0.8\%$ between
cohorts. Consequently, a few-pixel error can preserve AP50 while changing the
strict-IoU ordering that determines mAP, and an iterative detector must refine
many adjacent candidates without confusing their identities.

Conventional detectors offer complementary but incomplete solutions.
Anchor-based systems such as Cascade R-CNN~\cite{cai2018cascade} and
YOLOX~\cite{ge2021yolox} are fast, whereas transformer detectors such as
DINO~\cite{zhang2023dino} obtain strong localization through multi-scale
deformable attention~\cite{zhu2021deformabledetr}. DiffusionDet~\cite{chen2023diffusiondet} instead treats boxes as a
set generated from noise, an appealing formulation for variable spatial
layouts, but its DDPM inheritance introduces a curved stochastic path and a
many-step design that increase the cost of time-sensitive microscopy analysis.
More importantly, simply shortening that path does not resolve two
detection-specific mismatches: multistep integration assumes a persistent
state identity, whereas proposal renewal replaces selected states; and final
ranking by class confidence ignores whether a small chromosome is localized
accurately enough to survive strict IoU thresholds.

Rectified Flow~\cite{liu2023rectified} (RF) offers a natural generation
foundation by replacing the DDPM path with a deterministic linear transport
between noise boxes and targets. Applying RF to

detection raises three linked questions: how to learn a well-conditioned box
trajectory from limited data, how to integrate it without violating proposal
identity, and how to rank fixed outputs by both semantic and geometric
reliability. KaryoFlow organizes the answer as Generation, Decision, and
Deployment, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:method_overview}. Its contributions are:

First, we adapt few-step box transport to high-instance-count metaphase
detection. The generation stage combines RF in normalized four-dimensional box
space, a direct data-prediction objective, and identity-consistent multistep
integration. We show that replacing a proposal invalidates the history secant
unless its history is reset, providing a design principle for crowded box-set
refinement. RF and DPM-Solver++~\cite{lu2022dpmpp} are adopted foundations; the
contribution is their task-specific formulation and analysis.

Second, we introduce Localization-Quality Calibrated Ranking (LQCR) for the
strict-IoU bottleneck encountered after class confidence has nearly saturated.
LQCR learns localization reliability from final proposal features and ranks
detections by $s=pq^2$. It is applied only when outputs are emitted, leaving
candidate coordinates, class scores, proposal survival, and the RF trajectory
unchanged before final post-processing. Here, calibration denotes calibration
of the ranking score to localization quality, not probabilistic calibration.

Third, the deployment layer separates solver evaluations from cascade-stage
evaluations and compresses them through few-step integration, head
distillation, and proposal pruning. We evaluate all three layers on two public
chromosome datasets containing $2{,}200$ and $5{,}000$ images using independent
training runs, paired component comparisons, held-out tests, strict-IoU
analysis, and standardized latency profiling.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}


\paragraph{Chromosome detection and karyotyping}
A metaphase image combines several difficulties rarely present together in
natural-image detection: dozens of adjacent objects, homologous classes with
similar morphology, scale variation between chromosome groups, staining
shift, and incomplete boundaries caused by touching or overlap. Earlier work
progressed from joint segmentation and classification
\cite{schwartzkopf2005mfish,qin2019varifocalchromosome} to learned enumeration,
straightening, and metaphase detection
\cite{xiao2020deepacev2,li2024mcvae,kuo2024chromosomenet}. Recent 2026 studies
confirm that the clinical workflow is moving toward direct detection from
whole metaphase images: object detectors remove manual single-chromosome
cropping~\cite{matsumoto2026scc}, and integrated pipelines combine YOLO-based
detection with pairing and anomaly screening~\cite{rosas2026karyotyping}.
However, those reports emphasize concordance, mAP50, or downstream workflow
accuracy. These measures can remain high when a small chromosome has a
several-pixel boundary error. Transfer-learning failure analysis likewise
identifies overlap-related cropping artifacts as a persistent source of
chromosome confusion~\cite{chen2026transfer}. KaryoFlow therefore targets the
localization stage itself, with mAP over IoU $0.50{:}0.95$, strict-IoU
diagnostics, and measured inference cost.

Large-scale representation learning addresses a complementary limitation.
CHROMA was pretrained on more than four million chromosome images and improves
robustness to rare abnormalities and challenging acquisition conditions
\cite{yang2026chroma}. Such single-chromosome representations reduce annotation
burden after extraction, but do not resolve how densely overlapping candidates
should move, retain identity, or be ranked in the preceding box-detection
stage. This distinction motivates a detector specialized for metaphase box
geometry rather than another isolated-chromosome classifier.

\paragraph{Generative detection and few-step integration}
DiffusionDet~\cite{chen2023diffusiondet} formulates object detection as
iterative box denoising. FlowDet~\cite{baty2025flowdet} and
DeFloMat~\cite{deflomat2025} replace DDPM-style dynamics with conditional flow
matching or RF, while DiffuBox~\cite{chen2024diffubox} studies a distinct 3D
refinement setting. RF~\cite{liu2023rectified} and Flow
Matching~\cite{lipman2023flow} provide our adopted continuous-time foundation,
and DPM-Solver++~\cite{lu2022dpmpp} supplies the multistep integration
principle. Recent work has also explored flow-matching design and optimized
priors for cellular-microscopy generation and medical augmentation
\cite{jones2026flowmatching,nutzel2026flowmatching}; these image-generative
settings are complementary to our discriminative transport of box sets.
Existing studies emphasize the probability path or solver, but do
not analyze the interaction between solver history and proposal renewal.
KaryoFlow derives the data-prediction update for the RF box path and makes
proposal identity an explicit condition of multistep inference. Diffusion in
TMI has mainly addressed synthesis, segmentation, or robust classification
\cite{tivnan2025fourierdiffusion,yang2025dgddm,shen2025ladine}, leaving
few-step medical box-set prediction comparatively underexplored.

\paragraph{Localization quality in medical detection}
Medical detectors must handle small targets, incomplete labels, acquisition
shift, and structured false positives. Representative TMI studies address
few-shot pulmonary lesions, heterogeneous lesion annotations, and relational
transfer across domains~\cite{yang2024ptbnet,yan2021universal,chen2023graphskt},
while context refinement, weak supervision, and imbalanced learning reduce
task-specific false positives~\cite{ren2023ipsilateral,wang2019weaklesion,bae2015polyp}.
For general detection, IoU-Net predicts localization confidence for proposal
selection~\cite{jiang2018iounet}, and GFL and VarifocalNet combine class and
localization quality in dense heads~\cite{li2020gfl,zhang2021vfnet}. LQCR
inherits the probability-ranking motivation but differs in scope: it is
applied only after a generative detector has completed all trajectory updates,
so solver history, coordinates, predicted classes, and proposal survival are
fixed. This controlled boundary is particularly useful in chromosome images,
where AP50 is nearly saturated but strict-IoU errors remain concentrated in
small and overlapping instances. The emphasis on output reliability is also
consistent with evidence that accurate medical networks can remain poorly
calibrated~\cite{mehrtash2020calibration}.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

KaryoFlow separates three roles that are easy to conflate in iterative
detection. The \emph{Generation} layer transports a persistent set of noisy
boxes to chromosome boxes. The \emph{Decision} layer ranks the completed
candidates by semantic and localization reliability. The \emph{Deployment}
layer reduces solver and cascade-head computation without changing either
role.

\subsection{Generation: Few-Step Identity-Consistent Box Transport}

\subsubsection{Box-Space Rectified Flow}\label{sec:method-rf}

\paragraph{Linear transport in chromosome-box space}
Each chromosome is represented by a normalized center and size vector
$(c_x,c_y,w,h)$. Training connects a Gaussian source box $\mathbf{x}_1$ to a
matched chromosome box $\mathbf{x}_0$ through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-path}
  \mathbf{x}_t = (1-t)\, \mathbf{x}_0 + t\, \mathbf{x}_1, \qquad t \in [0,1],
\end{equation}
whose target velocity is $\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0$. Unlike image diffusion,
only four box coordinates are transported; the microscopy features remain
fixed conditioning information. This low-dimensional, straight path is a
natural match for a metaphase spread, where approximately $46$ boxes must move
concurrently while preserving their individual identities.

\paragraph{Time conditioning}
AdaLN-Zero~\cite{dhariwal2021diffusion} injects the time embedding into each
proposal head through zero-initialized residual modulation. It is retained as
a stable conditioning mechanism; controlled ablation does not support treating
it as a separate accuracy contribution.

\subsubsection{Stable Parameterization and Few-Step Integration}\label{sec:method-solver}

\paragraph{Few-step integration}
Euler integration provides the first-order reference: it is inexpensive per
step but uses only the current box estimate. Heun adds a predictor--corrector
evaluation to reduce local integration error, so four reverse steps require
seven network evaluations. DPM-Solver++~\cite{lu2022dpmpp} instead reaches
second order by reusing the previous target-box prediction, requiring only one
new evaluation per step. We compare the solvers in this progression---Euler,
Heun, then DPM-Solver++---and attribute solver gains only under matched
trained models and step counts.

The model directly predicts $\mathbf{x}_0$ (the target box), and the update uses polynomial
interpolation of the $\mathbf{x}_0$ history in $t$ space (not the log-SNR
$\lambda$ space of VP-SDE). The RF-ODE in data-prediction form is
$d\mathbf{x}/dt = (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0(t))/t$; integrating exactly over
$[t_n, t_{n+1}]$ with linear $\mathbf{x}_0(t)$ interpolation yields the update in~(\ref{eq:dpmpp})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dpmpp}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{x}_{t_{n+1}} &= \tfrac{t_{n+1}}{t_n}\,\mathbf{x}_{t_n}
    + \bigl(1 - \tfrac{t_{n+1}}{t_n}\bigr)\,\mathbf{x}_0^{(n)}
    + \varphi_1\,\mathbf{D}_1, \\
  \varphi_1 &= t_{n+1}\log\tfrac{t_n}{t_{n+1}} - t_n + t_{n+1},\;
  \mathbf{D}_1 = \tfrac{\mathbf{x}_0^{(n)} - \mathbf{x}_0^{(n-1)}}{t_n - t_{n-1}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first two terms move a proposal toward its current box estimate; the last
term corrects that move using the change between the two most recent estimates.
The initial noise sample remains fixed. Thus four steps require four rather
than seven network evaluations. The endpoint is protected with
$t_{n+1}>10^{-7}$.

\paragraph{Why direct data prediction is numerically preferable here}
For the path in~\eqref{eq:rf-path},
$\mathbf{x}_0=\mathbf{x}_t-t\mathbf{v}$. Hence the two parameterizations carry
the same information, but their pointwise squared errors satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:x0-v-weight}
 \mathcal{L}_{v}(t)=t^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{x_0}(t).
\end{equation}
With the shifted schedule, velocity regression therefore amplifies gradient
noise as $t\rightarrow0$, where accurate terminal boxes matter most. Direct
$x_0$ prediction removes this avoidable conditioning factor. We use this
parameterization for its better-conditioned terminal objective, without
claiming a test-set accuracy gain over algebraically equivalent velocity
prediction.

\subsubsection{History-Consistent Proposal Integration}
\label{sec:method-engineering}

\paragraph{Proposal identity is a premise of multistep history}
Let $r_i^n=1$ indicate that proposal row $i$ is renewed after step $n$.
\begin{proposition}[Identity consistency]\label{prop:identity}
The multistep correction $\mathbf{D}_{1,i}$ in~\eqref{eq:dpmpp} is a
consistent first-order approximation to a trajectory derivative only if the
two states indexed by row $i$ belong to the same proposal trajectory, or if
the row-wise history is reset after replacement.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Without renewal, differentiability of $\mathbf{x}_{0,i}(t)$ gives the standard
secant limit
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{D}_{1,i}=
 \frac{\mathbf{x}_{0,i}^{(n)}-\mathbf{x}_{0,i}^{(n-1)}}{t_n-t_{n-1}}
 \longrightarrow \frac{d\mathbf{x}_{0,i}}{dt}.
\end{equation}
If $r_i^n=1$, the numerator subtracts predictions from two independently
initialized states. Its limit contains a finite cross-trajectory jump divided
by $t_n-t_{n-1}$ and therefore is not the derivative along either trajectory.
Resetting history removes the invalid correction. \qedhere
\end{proof}
We use the simpler alternative of disabling renewal after verifying sufficient
proposal redundancy. This choice is well matched to metaphase images: the
object count is tightly concentrated near $46$, so $K=500$ provides multiple
candidates per chromosome, while maintaining identity avoids mixing adjacent,
morphologically similar instances. In the prespecified Dataset~2 evaluation,
disabling renewal at $K=100$ costs $0.0169$ mAP relative to retaining renewal,
showing that history consistency cannot compensate for inadequate capacity.

\subsection{Decision: Localization-Quality-Aware Ranking}\label{sec:method-lqcr}

Chromosome classes can be predicted confidently even when their boxes are a
few pixels too loose or shifted. For small chromosomes, a few-pixel
displacement produces a large IoU change; for touching chromosomes,
neighboring candidates can have similar class confidence despite different
localization quality.
For proposal feature $F$, correct-class event $C$, IoU $U$, and evaluation
threshold $\tau$, the true-positive probability separates as
\begin{equation}
 P(C{=}1,U{\geq}\tau\mid F)
 =P(C{=}1\mid F)P(U{\geq}\tau\mid C{=}1,F).
 \label{eq:lqcr-factorization}
\end{equation}
The second factor motivates augmenting class confidence with localization
information. LQCR does not estimate this threshold event probability directly;
instead, it learns continuous IoU as a ranking surrogate through a two-layer
quality branch $q=\sigma(g_\phi(F))$ attached only to the final cascade stage.
It adds $33{,}153$ parameters. The backbone, FPN, proposal
interaction, class head, and box head are frozen during branch fitting, so the
experiment isolates the ranking-stage effect from box generation.
Here calibration means alignment of the detection ranking with localization
quality; it is not presented as calibrated posterior probability.

For Hungarian-matched proposal $i$, let $m_i=1$ and let $u_i$ be the detached
aligned IoU; unmatched proposals have $m_i=0$. We train the scalar logit with
the continuous target $y_i=m_iu_i$ and weighted binary cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal{L}_{q}&=\frac{\lambda_q}{\max(N_+,1)}
 \sum_i w_i\,\ell_{\mathrm{BCE}}(g_i,y_i),\\
 w_i&=m_iu_i+(1-m_i)\alpha q_i^{\gamma},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:quality-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_q=0.25$, $\alpha=0.75$, and $\gamma=2$. Because the detector
weights are frozen and the IoU targets are detached, fitting the quality branch
cannot update predicted box geometry. Matched proposals are supervised toward
their aligned IoU, whereas unmatched proposals receive a zero target. At output,
class probability $p$ is
replaced by $s=pq^2$. The exponent is an empirically selected ranking transform,
not a consequence of~\eqref{eq:lqcr-factorization}. A validation sweep over
$\beta\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2\}$ selects the exponent $2$; this learned-quality
sweep is separate from the true-IoU upper bound used only to quantify headroom.

The final-only design keeps raw class scores in the solver,
proposal renewal, Top-$K$ pruning, and early stopping. Calibration is applied
only when emitting detections, so boxes, predicted classes, and RF trajectories
remain fixed at the candidate level; score-dependent NMS or max-detection
selection may consequently choose a different final subset. All detector
parameters are frozen while the quality branch is fitted, isolating ranking
from box generation. This design targets the observed chromosome regime in
which AP50 is nearly saturated while substantial error remains at strict IoU
thresholds. Figure~\ref{fig:lqcr-principle} summarizes the ranking principle,
its final-stage operating scope, and the paired fixed-model effect.

\subsection{Deployment: Orthogonal Inference Compression}
\label{sec:method-compression}

\paragraph{Cascade-head distillation}
Solver NFE counts network calls, whereas each call evaluates $H$ cascade heads.
We distill a six-stage teacher into a three-stage student with stage-aligned
feature and prediction supervision. At
four solver steps this reduces cascade-head evaluations (CHE) from 24 to 12,
while NFE remains four; the reduction is therefore 24-to-12 CHE, not NFE.
The compressed model reaches $0.8595$ versus
$0.8598$ and reduces RTX A6000 latency from $75.20$ to $44.10$ ms.
Together with few-step integration, this reduces latency from the
$120.34$-ms RF+Heun reference to $44.10$ ms, a $2.73\times$ acceleration;
the $0.0003$ mAP cost is measured against the six-stage teacher under the same
three-inference-seed test protocol.


\paragraph{Top-$K$ proposal pruning}\label{sec:method-topk}
After step 0 of inference, we prune proposals from 500 to $K$ based on
confidence scores; only the top-$K$ proposals proceed through steps 1--3.
Because pruning changes the proposal set, multistep history is reset before
the retained proposals continue refinement.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Datasets}
Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarizes the two public chromosome datasets,
referred to as Dataset~1 and Dataset~2. Dataset~1 is Chromosome20240904
(RST)~\cite{rst_dataset}, with $2{,}200$ images; Dataset~2 is the
24 Chromosomes Object dataset~\cite{tseng2023dataset,lu2022cil54816}, with
$5{,}000$ images. Their training splits contain $1{,}540$ and $3{,}500$
images, respectively. Both have a median of $46$ annotated instances per
image, but their median relative box areas differ substantially
($0.4\%$ versus $0.8\%$). Under the COCO absolute-area definition,
$23.0\%$ of Dataset~1 instances are small, compared with only $0.4\%$

statistics quantify the small, crowded regime and the scale shift used for
cross-dataset replication. Both datasets use image-level random splitting.
A single blood sample may yield multiple metaphase images, but patient
identifiers are unavailable; thus patient-level separation cannot be verified.
We analyze only the publicly released images and annotations and collect no
additional human-subject data.

\paragraph{Architecture and training}
Our base framework (KaryoFlow) uses ResNet-50~\cite{he2016resnet} + FPN~\cite{lin2017fpn}
(256 channels, 4 levels), 500 proposals, 6 cascade transformer heads with
deep supervision (5 auxiliary heads). Optimization: AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}
(learning rate $5{\times}10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), 5-epoch warmup + CosineAnnealing~\cite{loshchilov2017sgdr},
150 epochs. Loss: Focal~\cite{lin2017focal} ($\lambda_{\text{cls}}{=}2$) +
L1 ($\lambda{=}5$) + GIoU~\cite{rezatofighi2019giou} ($\lambda{=}2$) with
Hungarian matching~\cite{kuhn1955assignment}. Diffusion uses RF with shifted
noise schedule (shift$=3$); default solver is DPM-Solver++ 4-step.
For the isolated LQCR intervention, the trained six-stage detector initializes the
model, all pre-existing parameters are frozen, and only the 33k-parameter
quality branch is optimized for at most 12 epochs with AdamW learning rate
$10^{-3}$ and cosine decay; model selection uses validation performance. Thus
this phase fits a ranking statistic but cannot alter the detector's generated
boxes or class logits.

\paragraph{Detection protocol}
Boxes are represented in two spaces: image space (absolute pixel xyxy) for
RoIAlign and NMS, and diffusion space (cxcywh normalized to $[0,1]$, mapped
to $[-s, +s]$, $s{=}2$). The RF forward path is $x_t = (1{-}t)\,x_0 +
t\,\varepsilon$ (DDPM baseline uses cosine schedule). At inference, 500
random-noise proposals are iteratively denoised; with time-ensemble,
predictions from all steps are concatenated and deduplicated by per-class
NMS (IoU $0.5$). Evaluation uses COCO mAP$@0.50{:}0.95$ (10 IoU thresholds,
$\text{maxDets}{=}100$).
Validation data are used only for model and hyperparameter selection.
Unless explicitly identified as a diagnostic upper bound, every final detector
comparison, post-selection hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, and accuracy coordinate in an
efficiency plot is evaluated on the held-out test cohort (220 images for
Dataset~1 and 1,000 for Dataset~2). Accuracy evaluation is hardware independent
under the fixed software and seed protocol; all latency and FPS measurements
use the same RTX A6000.

\paragraph{Statistical considerations}
Three independently trained detectors with distinct recorded framework seeds
are evaluated on each dataset.
LQCR is fitted separately to each frozen parent detector and evaluated with
the same inference seed within every pair. Fixed-checkpoint inference-seed
means remain only in mechanism and efficiency analyses, where they quantify
proposal-initialization variation rather than training uncertainty.

\paragraph{Reproducibility}
All reported comparisons retain their dataset split, random seed, trained
model, and evaluation protocol. Controlled results are separated from
exploratory runs and diagnostic upper bounds. For LQCR, the base detector is
frozen and learned-quality sweeps are distinguished from analyses using true
IoU. This protocol prevents unmatched training conditions or unattainable
upper bounds from entering model claims.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
KaryoFlow's components address different consequences of metaphase-image
structure. The low-dimensional linear path and direct box prediction avoid
explicit terminal error amplification and motivate a stable box-space
parameterization, although no separate accuracy gain is claimed. Proposal identity
matters because dozens of adjacent candidates evolve simultaneously, while the
nearly fixed chromosome count permits a redundant proposal pool that makes
renewal unnecessary at the retained operating point. LQCR then addresses the
remaining decision bottleneck: class confidence is already sufficient at IoU
$0.5$, but it does not identify which lower-area or overlapping boxes will survive
stricter localization thresholds. The concentration of LQCR gains at AP90 and
AP95 is consistent with this task-derived mechanism.

The efficiency results reveal complementary deployment controls rather than a
single opaque speedup. DPM-Solver++ reduces solver evaluations, distillation
reduces cascade-head evaluations, and Top-$K$ reduces proposals after the first
step. This decomposition enables an
accuracy-oriented setting ($0.8668$ mAP, $77.68$ ms) or a compressed setting
($0.8595$ mAP, $44.10$ ms) without changing the central trajectory--ranking
formulation.

The study has three boundaries. First, the method addresses chromosome
detection within automated karyotyping; segmentation, straightening, pairing,
and abnormality diagnosis are outside its present scope. Both cohorts are public chromosome
datasets and lack patient identifiers, so patient-level leakage and clinical
workflow outcomes cannot be assessed. Second, LQCR is evaluated through three
independently trained parent--child pairs on each dataset, but three runs
provide descriptive variability rather than a high-powered significance test.
Natural-image validation is required to establish broader generality.
Third, history-consistent inference relies on proposal redundancy: the observed
$K=100$ failure should be treated as an operating constraint, not hidden by the
high-$K$ result. These limits do not affect the box-path derivations, but they
define the present empirical scope.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
KaryoFlow aligns trajectory learning, multistep state identity, and final
localization-aware ranking for dense chromosome detection. RF supplies the
few-step generative foundation; direct data prediction provides a
well-conditioned terminal objective; and the proposal-identity analysis makes DPM++
history mathematically coherent at the retained proposal count. The
decision-stage contribution, LQCR, improves paired test mAP by $0.0067$ across
three Dataset~1 runs and by $0.0065$ across three Dataset~2 runs. In the
fixed-detector mechanism analysis, Dataset~2 AP90/AP95 improve by
$0.0278/0.0300$ without changing candidate boxes before ranking.
Together with orthogonal inference compression, these results establish a
competitive and interpretable generative detector for chromosome karyotyping.
